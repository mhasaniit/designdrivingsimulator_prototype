# Wet Road Driving Simulator Study

**Course:** GEM 380 Interaction Design Workshop Research/Design Project  
**Study title:** Wet-Road Driving Simulator: Feedback and Safer Decision-Making

[Play the prototype](https://mhasaniit.github.io/designdrivingsimulator_prototype/)

## What is this study about?

This student research and design project explores how people use information presented in a short, screen-based driving scenario. The prototype depicts a rainy road and a vehicle ahead. The study considers how participants respond to the interface and whether its instructions and feedback are understandable. The specific study comparison is explained after the session.

## What will participants do?

A facilitated study session is expected to take approximately **20–25 minutes**. Planned activities include a short pre-study survey, instructions, a choice in a wet-road scenario, prototype feedback, a matched repeat prompt, and a post-study survey. Participants do not operate a real vehicle.

The public prototype is an interactive screenshot demonstration. The facilitated study activities, surveys, matched repeat prompt, and feedback assignment are managed separately by the study team.

## What information is collected?

During facilitated sessions, the researcher observes gameplay in person and writes notes using a study code. Study materials may include survey responses and choices from the baseline and repeat prompts.

**The study does not use audio recording, video recording, photographs, or screen recording. The researcher does not capture participants' screens.**

The prototype does not include a built-in survey or study-response submission system.

## Risks and possible benefits

The activity may cause mild frustration when a screen or task is unclear. Thinking about a driving hazard or reconsidering a decision may also feel uncomfortable. Participants may skip questions, request a break, or stop.

There may be no direct benefit to participants. Findings may help improve the clarity and usefulness of the prototype's feedback.

## Voluntary participation and withdrawal

Participation is voluntary. Participants may skip questions or stop at any time without penalty. Requests to remove study-code-linked data can be made to the study team using the participant's study code while the data can still be located. Removal may no longer be possible after data has been combined into summaries without a way to identify individual responses.

## Privacy and data handling

Study surveys and observation notes use study codes rather than participant names. Signed consent or permission forms are stored separately from study responses. Storage, authorized access, and retention arrangements are described in the study's participant materials.

Class presentations and reports may use aggregate findings or de-identified examples. Participant names, direct identifiers, consent forms, and individual study responses should not be uploaded to this public repository.

## Questions

For study questions, contact the study team or course instructor through the course's official communication channels. Session dates, locations, and participation details are provided separately.

## Using the prototype

1. Select **Start drive**, choose **Sedan**, and continue.
2. Choose **Dawn, Morning, Afternoon, Evening, or Night**.
3. Read the controls and begin three highway decisions: slowing traffic, an occupied passing lane, and an approaching exit.
4. **Hold and release** the brake or accelerator pedal to change speed. Select the steering wheel to attempt an overtake, or choose Maintain current speed.
5. Review feedback after each response. Practice retries preserve the first attempt in the summary.
6. Review safer driving benefits, then finish on the original Drive complete screen.

### Controls

| Action | Mouse / touch | Keyboard |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Brake | Hold brake pedal, then release | Hold B or Space |
| Accelerate | Hold accelerator pedal, then release | Hold A or Up arrow |
| Attempt overtake | Select steering wheel | O or Left arrow |

There is no countdown. The drive begins at **70 mph**, with separate situations beginning at 65 and 60 mph. These are illustrative starting speeds, not recommended speeds for rain. Respond to the traffic and watch the speed display. Feedback uses scenario-specific speed ranges; it is not a driving-safety assessment.

The prototype uses generated highway artwork for all five times of day, plus a realistic steering wheel and pedal controls. Feedback and the summary are interactive HTML overlays on the selected scene. It does not model vehicle physics, collisions, or real steering. Braking is appropriate for these specific hazards and is not the answer to every driving situation.

Choices stay in memory in the current browser session. No analytics, survey submission, recording, or response upload is built into the prototype.

To run locally, download and extract the repository, keep the image files beside `index.html`, and open `index.html` in a browser. No installation is required.
